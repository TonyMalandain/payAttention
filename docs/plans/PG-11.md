# Plan — PG-11: changing the travel time from an alert is obvious and readable

Backlog: PG-11 (refines R-11, "in-person meetings count down to the leave-by time"). Spec: `docs/prd/meeting-guardian.md` §4 Travel time. Evidence: the owner's first real in-person alert, 2026-09-30.
Architecture: `boundaries.md` (ports and adapters, ADR-0011; "UI must not decide … whether to show something"), `interfaces.md` §2 (`plan` and its `env`) and §3 (Controller ↔ UI, the `dispatch` actions), `overview.md` "Time and escalation model" (the "Visible?" rule), ADR-0002 (plan is level-triggered and pure), ADR-0004 (own chrome; the card never takes key focus).

**This plan has two parts that land as two separate changes.**
- **Part A** (readable boxes, a real button, the fit check, screenshots) is `ui/` and stylesheet only. **It may start now.**
- **Part B** (the heads-up stays while the boxes are shown) changes the escalation model's "Visible?" rule and two documented internal contracts. **It must not start until the architect accepts the design below** and updates `interfaces.md` §2/§3 and `overview.md`. See "For the architect".

## What changes, and for whom

For the calendar owner, on the heads-up (step 1) and nudge (step 2) card of an in-person meeting:
- The travel line reads "Travel: 30 min (default)" with a **Change travel time** button next to it, drawn like the card's other secondary buttons (today: an underlined "· change" link).
- The button opens the boxes 10, 15, 20, 30, 45, 60, 90 min, on two lines (four, then three). Every box reads "10 min" … "90 min" in full on both cards; the current value keeps its amber mark.
- Picking a box saves it for the place and moves the alert times at once, exactly as today (`setTravel`). It is not an acknowledgement.
- (Part B) While the boxes are shown, the heads-up does not hide after its display time.

## Why the boxes are unreadable today (measured, not assumed)

- `extension/ui/alertContent.js` `_updateTravel` puts seven `St.Button`s with the class `payattention-button` (padding 10px 20px, 13pt bold) in **one** horizontal `St.BoxLayout`. The PM's lead is correct.
- St applies `max-width` to the content box, so the step-1 card's content is at most **380 px** (step 2: 460 px).
- Label widths, Pango at 96 dpi, Adwaita Sans Bold 13pt (the owner's interface font, `Adwaita Sans 11`, scaling 1.0): "10 min" 55 px, "15 min" 54 px, the rest 59 px; together **404 px**.
- One row needs 7 × 40 px of padding + 6 × 10 px of spacing = 340 px before any text. That leaves about 40 px for seven labels. `St.Label` ellipsizes by default, so each shrinks to "…" or nothing. This is exactly what the owner saw.
- **No single row can hold seven readable "N min" boxes in 380 px** (at 10pt bold the labels alone are 306 px). So the boxes wrap to two lines. The ticket keeps the boxes and their labels, not the single line; the owner's condition was "as long as the numbers are readable".
- Measured for the new layout at 13pt bold:
  - four boxes with 12 px side padding: 4 × 83 + 3 × 10 = **362 px ≤ 380**;
  - travel line: "Travel: 30 min (default)" at 11pt is 163 px, plus 10 px, plus the button ("Change travel time" 159 px + 40 px) = **372 px ≤ 380**.

## The component

- **Responsible for (`extension/ui/alertContent.js`):** drawing the content of a card or takeover panel from an `AlertView` and reporting clicks. It decides nothing.
- **Has drifted by:** one piece of state. `_travelOpen` is a UI-held "are the boxes open" flag. Today that is harmless display state. Once the heads-up must stay while the boxes are open (Part B), it becomes an input to *whether the card is shown*, and the UI may not decide that. Part B moves it to the controller. Part A leaves it where it is.
- **Part A belongs here because:** how the travel line and the boxes are laid out, and how they are styled, is pure drawing. No step, deadline, value list or visibility changes.
- **Part B belongs in `core/plan.js` + `app/controller.js` + `app/view.js`**, not in `ui/`:
  - whether a step-1 card is visible is decided in one place, `plan()` (ADR-0002; `overview.md` "Visible?"). The hold is one more term in that rule, so it goes there;
  - "the boxes are open for this card" is owner-driven transient state. The controller already holds the other owner-driven state and routes actions, so it holds this, in memory only (not alert state: nothing to persist; after a shell restart the card comes back with the boxes closed);
  - `view.js` already resolves every "what to show", so it tells the UI whether the boxes are open.

  Keeping the card up from `ui/` (ignoring `view.alert === null` while the boxes are open) is rejected: the UI would decide whether to show something, and would draw a card the plan says is gone.

# Part A — readable boxes and a real button  (may start now)

## Seams (Part A)

No exported signature, action or View field changes. Inside `extension/ui/alertContent.js`:

- New module constant, above the class:
  ```js
  // Four 13pt "90 min" boxes (83 px each) fit the heads-up card's 380 px; seven do not.
  const TRAVEL_CHOICES_PER_ROW = 4;
  ```
- New module-private function, next to `label()`:
  ```js
  /** A card button whose text is never ellipsized: a box too narrow grows the card instead. */
  function unclippedButton(name, text, styleClass)   // -> St.Button
  ```
  It builds `new St.Label({ text, x_align: Clutter.ActorAlign.CENTER, y_align: Clutter.ActorAlign.CENTER })`, sets `clutter_text.ellipsize = Pango.EllipsizeMode.NONE`, and returns `new St.Button({ name, style_class: styleClass, child: <that label>, can_focus: true })`.
  - **Misuse designed out:** the travel buttons have no path that ellipsizes. If the text is wider than the room, the label's minimum width forces the card wider (St never lets `max-width` go below the minimum). Text can grow the card, but it can never be cut. That also covers larger text-scaling settings.
- Actor names (for the smoke check and the screenshots; no style depends on them):
  - `payattention-travel-text`: the "Travel: …" label;
  - `payattention-travel-change`: the Change travel time button;
  - `payattention-travel-choices`: the vertical box holding the rows of boxes;
  - `payattention-travel-choice-<minutes>`: each box, for example `payattention-travel-choice-10`.

  None starts with `payattention-action-`, so the arming delay (`_arm`) and the existing `buttonsOffMonitors` check are unaffected. The travel buttons stay un-armed, as today: neither opening the boxes nor picking one ends the alert.

# Part B — the heads-up stays while the boxes are shown  (blocked on the architect)

**Do not start Part B until the architect has accepted this design and updated `interfaces.md` §2 and §3 and `overview.md` "Visible?".** If the architect changes the shape, this part is re-planned. Part A does not depend on it.

## Seams (Part B)

1. **`core/plan.js` — `plan(now, judged, alertState, timing, env)`**, signature unchanged. `env` gains one **required** field (`core/types.js`):
   ```js
   /** @typedef {{userActive:boolean,dataHealthy:boolean,heldHeadsUpKey:string|null}} PlanEnv
    *  heldHeadsUpKey: the occurrence whose heads-up the owner is holding on screen (its travel choices are open), or null */
   ```
   - `checkInputs` throws `TypeError('plan: env.heldHeadsUpKey must be null or a non-empty string')` otherwise. Required, not optional, so a caller cannot forget it and silently lose the hold.
   - The step-1 rule becomes:
     ```js
     visible = !snoozed && (headsUpShownAt === null || now < headsUpShownAt + timing.headsUpDisplayMs || key === env.heldHeadsUpKey)
     ```
   - A snooze still wins. Steps 2–4 ignore the field. `recordHeadsUpShown` is unchanged.
2. **`app/controller.js` — `UiAction`** gains one member:
   ```js
   | {type: 'toggleTravelChoices', key: string}
   ```
   - A toggle, not "open: true/false", so the UI never has to know the current state and cannot send a stale one.
   - The controller holds `this._openTravelChoices: {key: string, step: 1|2} | null` (in memory, never stored) and `this._shownAlert: AlertView | null` (the last `view.alert`).
3. **`app/view.js` — `buildView(p)`** gains a required parameter `p.openTravelChoices: {key: string, step: number} | null`. `AlertView.travel` gains `choicesOpen: boolean`: true only when
   - `openTravelChoices` is not null,
   - its key and step equal the alert's, and
   - `choices.length > 0`.

   Update the `AlertView` typedef.
4. **`ui/alertContent.js`** loses `_travelOpen`. The boxes' `visible` is `travel.choicesOpen`. The change button dispatches `{ type: 'toggleTravelChoices', key: this._key }`. The UI holds no open/closed state at all.

**Misuse designed out:**
- The UI cannot keep a card up: visibility is decided only in `plan()`.
- A hold cannot outlive its card. The controller drops it on the same render in which the shown alert's key or step differs from the one it was opened on (ack, skip, snooze, step 2, another meeting taking the card, the meeting moving away after a pick).
- A hold for a card with no boxes cannot be created: the toggle is ignored unless the shown alert has that key and at least one choice.
- A monitor change rebuilds the card from the View, so the boxes come back in the same state instead of silently closing while the hold stays on.

## Behaviour decided here (from the PRD's plain reading; product may confirm)

- **The boxes stay open after a pick,** as today. The line then shows the new value, and the new mark is visible (PRD: "the line shows the new value"). The card therefore stays until the owner closes the boxes, acts, or the nudge replaces it (step 2 is always visible).
- **Closing the boxes** (Change travel time again) after the display time has run out lets the heads-up hide on that same tick. The PRD's rule covers only "while the boxes are shown"; there is no fresh display period.
- **A pick that moves the heads-up time into the future** (shorter travel) removes the card at once. So does a pick at step 2 that moves the meeting back to an already-shown step 1. This is today's behaviour, "the alert times move … at once", and is not changed.
