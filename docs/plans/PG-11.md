# Plan — PG-11: changing the travel time from an alert is obvious and readable

Backlog: PG-11 (refines R-11, "in-person meetings count down to the leave-by time"). Spec: `docs/prd/meeting-guardian.md` §4 Travel time. Evidence: the owner's first real in-person alert, 2026-09-30.
Architecture: `boundaries.md` (ports and adapters, ADR-0011; "UI must not decide … whether to show something"), `interfaces.md` §2 (`plan` and its `env`) and §3 (Controller ↔ UI, the `dispatch` actions), `overview.md` "Time and escalation model" (the "Visible?" rule), ADR-0002 (plan is level-triggered and pure), ADR-0004 (own chrome; the card never takes key focus).

**This plan has two parts that land as two separate changes.**
- **Part A** (readable boxes, a real button, the fit check, screenshots) is `ui/` and stylesheet only. **It may start now.**
- **Part B** (the heads-up stays while the boxes are shown) changes the escalation model's "Visible?" rule and two documented internal contracts. **It must not start until the architect accepts the design below** and updates `interfaces.md` §2/§3 and `overview.md`. See "For the architect".

## What changes, and for whom

For the calendar owner, on the heads-up (step 1) and nudge (step 2) card of an in-person meeting:
- The travel line reads "Travel: 30 min (default)" with a **Change travel time** button next to it, drawn like the card's other secondary buttons (today: an underlined "· change" link).
- The button opens the boxes 10, 15, 20, 30, 45, 60, 90 min, on two lines (four, then three). Every box reads "10 min" … "90 min" in full on both cards; the current value keeps its amber mark.
- Picking a box saves it for the place and moves the alert times at once, exactly as today (`setTravel`). It is not an acknowledgement.
- (Part B) While the boxes are shown, the heads-up does not hide after its display time.

## Why the boxes are unreadable today (measured, not assumed)

- `extension/ui/alertContent.js` `_updateTravel` puts seven `St.Button`s with the class `payattention-button` (padding 10px 20px, 13pt bold) in **one** horizontal `St.BoxLayout`. The PM's lead is correct.
- St applies `max-width` to the content box, so the step-1 card's content is at most **380 px** (step 2: 460 px).
- Label widths, Pango at 96 dpi, Adwaita Sans Bold 13pt (the owner's interface font, `Adwaita Sans 11`, scaling 1.0): "10 min" 55 px, "15 min" 54 px, the rest 59 px; together **404 px**.
- One row needs 7 × 40 px of padding + 6 × 10 px of spacing = 340 px before any text. That leaves about 40 px for seven labels. `St.Label` ellipsizes by default, so each shrinks to "…" or nothing. This is exactly what the owner saw.
- **No single row can hold seven readable "N min" boxes in 380 px** (at 10pt bold the labels alone are 306 px). So the boxes wrap to two lines. The ticket keeps the boxes and their labels, not the single line; the owner's condition was "as long as the numbers are readable".
- Measured for the new layout at 13pt bold:
  - four boxes with 12 px side padding: 4 × 83 + 3 × 10 = **362 px ≤ 380**;
  - travel line: "Travel: 30 min (default)" at 11pt is 163 px, plus 10 px, plus the button ("Change travel time" 159 px + 40 px) = **372 px ≤ 380**.

## The component

- **Responsible for (`extension/ui/alertContent.js`):** drawing the content of a card or takeover panel from an `AlertView` and reporting clicks. It decides nothing.
- **Has drifted by:** one piece of state. `_travelOpen` is a UI-held "are the boxes open" flag. Today that is harmless display state. Once the heads-up must stay while the boxes are open (Part B), it becomes an input to *whether the card is shown*, and the UI may not decide that. Part B moves it to the controller. Part A leaves it where it is.
- **Part A belongs here because:** how the travel line and the boxes are laid out, and how they are styled, is pure drawing. No step, deadline, value list or visibility changes.
- **Part B belongs in `core/plan.js` + `app/controller.js` + `app/view.js`**, not in `ui/`:
  - whether a step-1 card is visible is decided in one place, `plan()` (ADR-0002; `overview.md` "Visible?"). The hold is one more term in that rule, so it goes there;
  - "the boxes are open for this card" is owner-driven transient state. The controller already holds the other owner-driven state and routes actions, so it holds this, in memory only (not alert state: nothing to persist; after a shell restart the card comes back with the boxes closed);
  - `view.js` already resolves every "what to show", so it tells the UI whether the boxes are open.

  Keeping the card up from `ui/` (ignoring `view.alert === null` while the boxes are open) is rejected: the UI would decide whether to show something, and would draw a card the plan says is gone.

# Part A — readable boxes and a real button  (may start now)

## Seams (Part A)

No exported signature, action or View field changes. Inside `extension/ui/alertContent.js`:

- New module constant, above the class:
  ```js
  // Four 13pt "90 min" boxes (83 px each) fit the heads-up card's 380 px; seven do not.
  const TRAVEL_CHOICES_PER_ROW = 4;
  ```
- New module-private function, next to `label()`:
  ```js
  /** A card button whose text is never ellipsized: a box too narrow grows the card instead. */
  function unclippedButton(name, text, styleClass)   // -> St.Button
  ```
  It builds `new St.Label({ text, x_align: Clutter.ActorAlign.CENTER, y_align: Clutter.ActorAlign.CENTER })`, sets `clutter_text.ellipsize = Pango.EllipsizeMode.NONE`, and returns `new St.Button({ name, style_class: styleClass, child: <that label>, can_focus: true })`.
  - **Misuse designed out:** the travel buttons have no path that ellipsizes. If the text is wider than the room, the label's minimum width forces the card wider (St never lets `max-width` go below the minimum). Text can grow the card, but it can never be cut. That also covers larger text-scaling settings.
- Actor names (for the smoke check and the screenshots; no style depends on them):
  - `payattention-travel-text`: the "Travel: …" label;
  - `payattention-travel-change`: the Change travel time button;
  - `payattention-travel-choices`: the vertical box holding the rows of boxes;
  - `payattention-travel-choice-<minutes>`: each box, for example `payattention-travel-choice-10`.

  None starts with `payattention-action-`, so the arming delay (`_arm`) and the existing `buttonsOffMonitors` check are unaffected. The travel buttons stay un-armed, as today: neither opening the boxes nor picking one ends the alert.

# Part B — the heads-up stays while the boxes are shown  (blocked on the architect)

**Do not start Part B until the architect has accepted this design and updated `interfaces.md` §2 and §3 and `overview.md` "Visible?".** If the architect changes the shape, this part is re-planned. Part A does not depend on it.

## Seams (Part B)

1. **`core/plan.js` — `plan(now, judged, alertState, timing, env)`**, signature unchanged. `env` gains one **required** field (`core/types.js`):
   ```js
   /** @typedef {{userActive:boolean,dataHealthy:boolean,heldHeadsUpKey:string|null}} PlanEnv
    *  heldHeadsUpKey: the occurrence whose heads-up the owner is holding on screen (its travel choices are open), or null */
   ```
   - `checkInputs` throws `TypeError('plan: env.heldHeadsUpKey must be null or a non-empty string')` otherwise. Required, not optional, so a caller cannot forget it and silently lose the hold.
   - The step-1 rule becomes:
     ```js
     visible = !snoozed && (headsUpShownAt === null || now < headsUpShownAt + timing.headsUpDisplayMs || key === env.heldHeadsUpKey)
     ```
   - A snooze still wins. Steps 2–4 ignore the field. `recordHeadsUpShown` is unchanged.
2. **`app/controller.js` — `UiAction`** gains one member:
   ```js
   | {type: 'toggleTravelChoices', key: string}
   ```
   - A toggle, not "open: true/false", so the UI never has to know the current state and cannot send a stale one.
   - The controller holds `this._openTravelChoices: {key: string, step: 1|2} | null` (in memory, never stored) and `this._shownAlert: AlertView | null` (the last `view.alert`).
3. **`app/view.js` — `buildView(p)`** gains a required parameter `p.openTravelChoices: {key: string, step: number} | null`. `AlertView.travel` gains `choicesOpen: boolean`: true only when
   - `openTravelChoices` is not null,
   - its key and step equal the alert's, and
   - `choices.length > 0`.

   Update the `AlertView` typedef.
4. **`ui/alertContent.js`** loses `_travelOpen`. The boxes' `visible` is `travel.choicesOpen`. The change button dispatches `{ type: 'toggleTravelChoices', key: this._key }`. The UI holds no open/closed state at all.

**Misuse designed out:**
- The UI cannot keep a card up: visibility is decided only in `plan()`.
- A hold cannot outlive its card. The controller drops it on the same render in which the shown alert's key or step differs from the one it was opened on (ack, skip, snooze, step 2, another meeting taking the card, the meeting moving away after a pick).
- A hold for a card with no boxes cannot be created: the toggle is ignored unless the shown alert has that key and at least one choice.
- A monitor change rebuilds the card from the View, so the boxes come back in the same state instead of silently closing while the hold stays on.

## Behaviour decided here (from the PRD's plain reading; product may confirm)

- **The boxes stay open after a pick,** as today. The line then shows the new value, and the new mark is visible (PRD: "the line shows the new value"). The card therefore stays until the owner closes the boxes, acts, or the nudge replaces it (step 2 is always visible).
- **Closing the boxes** (Change travel time again) after the display time has run out lets the heads-up hide on that same tick. The PRD's rule covers only "while the boxes are shown"; there is no fresh display period.
- **A pick that moves the heads-up time into the future** (shorter travel) removes the card at once. So does a pick at step 2 that moves the meeting back to an already-shown step 1. This is today's behaviour, "the alert times move … at once", and is not changed.

# Part D fix round 1 — the sample cannot reach the owner's bus or keyring, and its guard is not flaky

Added 2026-09-30, after review of 0b9d1a8. **Sensitive: the owner's session bus, keyring (Secret Service, KWallet), dconf and extensions folder.** Product priority: the owner's safety. The sample and the smoke may share only the display, the runtime folder (`XDG_RUNTIME_DIR`) and the system bus (testing-and-build.md, Layer 3b).

## What changes, and for whom

For the owner: `npm run sample` and `npm run smoke` can no longer reach their live session bus or start any keyring, whatever a caller of the isolation functions passes, and the smoke no longer goes red because their live shell saved state next to the extensions folder. Nothing under `extension/` changes.

## The three defects, and one measured fact each

1. `isolated_run` passes `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` through and sets no bus. With `DBUS_SESSION_BUS_ADDRESS` unset, GLib connects to `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/bus`: the owner's live bus (reviewer measured it). Its privacy today depends on every caller remembering `dbus-run-session`. Extras could also override `HOME`, `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR`, `GIO_USE_VFS`, `PATH`.
2. The extensions-folder guard uses `ls -la`, which lists `..` (`~/.local/share/gnome-shell`); the live shell changes its mtime whenever it saves `application_state` or notifications. The snapshot is also taken after the build and install instead of before.
3. `4-no-keyring` passes whether or not the masks exist: nothing in rules mode asks for secrets. And the blocklist was incomplete. Measured here on 2026-09-30, `/usr/share/dbus-1/services` has **seven** names that start a keyring daemon: `org.freedesktop.secrets`, `org.gnome.keyring`, `org.freedesktop.impl.portal.Secret` (all `gnome-keyring-daemon --start`, which with a shared runtime folder reaches the owner's running daemon through `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/keyring/control`), `org.kde.secretservicecompat`, `org.freedesktop.impl.portal.desktop.kwallet` (both `ksecretd`), `org.kde.kwalletd5`, `org.kde.kwalletd6` (both `kwalletd6`). A default `dbus-run-session` reads `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/dbus-1/services` first (empty here today, but shared with the owner), and offers 118 activatable names.

## Decision: an allowlist bus, owned by `isolated_run`

Masks are a blocklist, and the reviewer has already found three names it missed (four, counting KWallet). A blocklist fails open. Instead:

- **`isolated_run` always runs its command on its own private bus:** `dbus-run-session --config-file=<home>/bus/session.conf`. Callers no longer write `dbus-run-session` and cannot forget it. It refuses any nested bus launcher in the command, which would otherwise start a default bus with every standard service directory.
- **That config lists exactly one service directory, `<home>/bus/services`.** `isolated_run` fills it on every call with copies of the system service files for a fixed allowlist, `ISOLATED_BUS_SERVICES`. No `<standard_session_servicedirs/>` and no `<include>`/`<includedir>`, so no machine-local file can add a directory. The shared runtime service directory, `~/.local/share/dbus-1/services`, flatpak exports and every name not on the list (all keyrings, portals, GOA) cannot be activated. D-Bus answers `ServiceUnknown` at once. Measured: a relative `<servicedir>services</servicedir>` resolves against the config file's folder, works under SELinux enforcing, and `ListActivatableNames` then returns only `org.freedesktop.DBus` and the copied names. Unknown names fail with `org.freedesktop.DBus.Error.ServiceUnknown`.
- **The allowlist** is what the current smoke and sample logs show gnome-shell, EDS and ibus activating (`Activating service name=…` in `.smoke-home/shell.log` and `.sample-home/shell.log`), minus the portals, GNOME Online Accounts and `org.gnome.Identity`, which watches Kerberos credentials. The sample already ran green with `portal.Desktop` failing:
  ```bash
  ISOLATED_BUS_SERVICES=(
      org.gnome.evolution.dataserver.Sources5
      org.gnome.evolution.dataserver.Calendar8
      org.gnome.evolution.dataserver.AddressBook10
      org.gnome.Shell.CalendarServer
      org.gnome.Shell.Notifications
      org.gnome.Shell.Screencast
      org.gnome.Shell.Extensions
      org.freedesktop.impl.portal.PermissionStore
      org.freedesktop.portal.IBus
      org.a11y.Bus
  )
  ```
- **The checks never activate anything.** No check calls `StartServiceByName`: if a block were missing, that would start the very daemon it is meant to keep out, and the red run would touch the owner's keyring. The checks read `ListActivatableNames` instead. It lists names and starts nothing.
- The runtime folder stays shared, as Layer 3b accepts: the window needs it. This design makes that sharing harmless for keyrings, because nothing that uses `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/keyring` can be activated.

## The component

- **Responsible for (`scripts/isolated-shell.sh`):** keeping a throwaway gnome-shell off the owner's session bus, settings and data, and giving the scripts the means to prove it.
- **Has drifted by:** its privacy is not in its own hands. The private bus is left to each caller, the extras are a blocklist, and the only process clean-up (in `sample.sh`) is a command-line match that can kill the owner's own `tail -f`. This round moves all of that behind the seam.
- **This change belongs here because:** the bus, the environment and the "which processes are ours" marker are the isolation environment itself. The keyring tripwire list belongs in a test (`tests/smoke/`), not in the environment.

## Seams (fix round 1)

All in `scripts/isolated-shell.sh`. Signatures of the existing three are unchanged. What they accept is narrower.

```bash
# The only homes an isolated shell may use.
ISOLATED_HOMES=(.smoke-home .sample-home)
# The only names the private bus can start (above). Copied from /usr/share/dbus-1/services.
ISOLATED_BUS_SERVICES=( … )
# The only NAME=value extras isolated_run accepts.
ISOLATED_RUN_EXTRAS=(LANG WAYLAND_DISPLAY GTK_A11Y PAYATTENTION_SMOKE_HOME PAYATTENTION_SAMPLE_HOME)

isolated_calendar <home> <id> <name> <email>  < calendar.ics
isolated_run <home> <user-home> [NAME=value ...] -- command [args ...]

# Two equal listings mean nothing under <dir> was added, removed, replaced, resized or touched.
isolated_snapshot <dir>

# Stops (SIGTERM) every process of this user whose environment carries <home>'s isolation marker; prints how many.
isolated_stop <home>
```

Private helper (leading underscore, not for callers): `_isolated_home <home>` prints the canonical home `"$repo/$name"` or prints a refusal on stderr and returns 1. Rules, all required:
- the basename is exactly one of `ISOLATED_HOMES`;
- `dirname`, resolved with `pwd -P`, is the repository (`$ROOT` resolved);
- `<home>` is not a symlink.

Refusal text: `isolated: refusing <home>: an isolated home is <repo>/.smoke-home or <repo>/.sample-home`. `.git`, `.github`, `.vscode`, `.isolation-probe` and anything outside the repository are refused. **Misuse designed out:** no function can write into a directory that is not a throwaway home.

- **`isolated_install <home>`:** `h="$(_isolated_home "$1")" || return 1` first, before any `mkdir`. The rest is unchanged, including the zip check and its message.
- **`isolated_calendar <home> <id> <name> <email>`:** `_isolated_home` first. `<id>` must match `^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]*$`, else `isolated_calendar: refusing id '<id>'` and return 1. `<name>` and `<email>` must not contain a newline, else `isolated_calendar: refusing a name or email with a line break`. Nothing is written on refusal.
- **`isolated_run`:**
  - `_isolated_home` first.
  - `<user-home>` must start with `/`, else refuse.
  - `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` must be set in the caller, else `isolated_run: XDG_RUNTIME_DIR is not set` and return 1.
  - Each extra's name must be in `ISOLATED_RUN_EXTRAS`, else `isolated_run: refusing <NAME>: an isolated shell accepts only <the list>`. This replaces today's `case` blocklist. A malformed extra and a missing `--` keep today's messages.
  - Any command argument whose basename is `dbus-run-session`, `dbus-daemon`, `dbus-launch`, `dbus-broker` or `dbus-broker-launch` is refused: `isolated_run: refusing <arg>: isolated_run provides the private bus itself`.
  - All refusals happen before anything is written or run.
  - Then it writes the bus (below) and creates `<home>/state`. It runs, exactly:
    ```bash
    env -i PATH="$PATH" HOME="$user_home" USER="$USER" LOGNAME="${LOGNAME:-$USER}" \
        XDG_RUNTIME_DIR="$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR" \
        XDG_CONFIG_HOME="$h/config" XDG_DATA_HOME="$h/data" XDG_CACHE_HOME="$h/cache" XDG_STATE_HOME="$h/state" \
        GSETTINGS_BACKEND=keyfile GIO_USE_VFS=local PAYATTENTION_ISOLATED_HOME="$h" \
        "${extras[@]}" dbus-run-session --config-file="$h/bus/session.conf" -- "$@"
    ```
  - **The bus, written by `isolated_run` on every call** (preparation inside, so there is nothing to forget):
    - `rm -rf "$h/bus"`, then `mkdir -p "$h/bus/services"`.
    - For each name in `ISOLATED_BUS_SERVICES`: if `/usr/share/dbus-1/services/<name>.service` exists and has the line `Name=<name>` (`grep -qx`), copy it verbatim to `$h/bus/services/<name>.service`. Otherwise skip it silently, because such a name could not start today either.
    - Write `$h/bus/session.conf` from a heredoc, exactly this:
    ```xml
    <!DOCTYPE busconfig PUBLIC "-//freedesktop//DTD D-Bus Bus Configuration 1.0//EN"
     "http://www.freedesktop.org/standards/dbus/1.0/busconfig.dtd">
    <!-- Written by scripts/isolated-shell.sh: a private session bus that can start only the services in ./services. -->
    <busconfig>
      <type>session</type>
      <keep_umask/>
      <listen>unix:tmpdir=/tmp</listen>
      <auth>EXTERNAL</auth>
      <servicedir>services</servicedir>
      <policy context="default">
        <allow send_destination="*" eavesdrop="true"/>
        <allow eavesdrop="true"/>
        <allow own="*"/>
      </policy>
      <!-- the limit lines of /usr/share/dbus-1/session.conf, copied verbatim: max_incoming_bytes … max_match_rules_per_connection, max_replies_per_connection -->
    </busconfig>
    ```
    - The `<limit>` lines are the 15 in `/usr/share/dbus-1/session.conf`, typed into the heredoc with the same names and values, so bus behaviour matches today's (notably `service_start_timeout` 120000).
    - The relative `services` path means the repository path never needs XML escaping.
  - **Misuse designed out:** a caller cannot reach the owner's bus, cannot start a keyring or portal, cannot override any variable `isolated_run` sets, and cannot bypass the allowlist with a nested bus.
- **`isolated_snapshot <dir>`:**
  - If `<dir>` exists or is a symlink: `{ find "$dir" -printf '%P\t%y\t%i\t%s\t%T@\t%l\n' 2>&1 || true; } | LC_ALL=C sort`. Otherwise it prints `absent`.
  - `find` without `-L` does not follow symlinks, lists `<dir>` itself (empty `%P`: its mtime catches entries added or removed directly under it), and never lists `..`.
- **`isolated_stop <home>`:**
  - `_isolated_home` first.
  - For each pid of `pgrep -u "$(id -u)"`, if `grep -qzxF "PAYATTENTION_ISOLATED_HOME=$h" "/proc/$pid/environ" 2>/dev/null`, then `kill "$pid" 2>/dev/null`, and count it only when `kill` succeeds.
  - Prints the count to stdout. Always returns 0 after a valid home.
  - Bus-activated services inherit the marker from `dbus-run-session`'s environment. The owner's own processes, such as a `tail -f .sample-home/shell.log`, never carry it. **Misuse designed out:** clean-up cannot kill anything the sample did not start.
