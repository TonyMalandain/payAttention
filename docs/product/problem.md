# The problem

The person who owns the calendar misses meetings they could not afford to miss. They do not miss them because nothing warned them. They miss them because warnings arrive all day, from every tool, and they have learned to ignore all of them. A reminder for a client call looks the same as a reminder for a lunch placeholder, a "focus time" block, or a company all-hands with two hundred people. After enough of these, the eye stops reading. The one reminder that mattered disappears with the rest.

The cost is not spread evenly. Missing an optional meeting costs nothing. Missing an interview, a client call, a board review, or a meeting you organised costs trust, and sometimes money. Because it matters so much, the calendar owner does the filtering by hand. They add custom reminders to the few meetings that count and try to pick the right number of minutes each time. This is work done again for every important meeting, and it fails as soon as they forget one. The author describes this directly: "I have to carefully craft the reminders to notify me at the right time."

A normal reminder has two weaknesses. It shows once and then goes away. If it arrives while you are deep in work, in another call, or away from the desk, it is gone before you see it, and nothing happens after that. It also uses the same lead time for every meeting. Thirty minutes is too early for a video call, because you forget again before it starts. It is too late for a meeting across town, because you should already be on the road. The right moment to warn someone depends on whether they must travel.

What the calendar owner needs is not more reminders but fewer and stronger ones. Placeholders and meetings where nobody will notice your absence should make no noise at all. Meetings that do count should be impossible to miss. If the start draws near and the person has shown no sign that they are on their way, the warning should get harder to ignore, until they cannot ignore it.

If nothing changes, the pattern continues. The person either keeps crafting reminders by hand for every important meeting or accepts that they will now and then miss one that matters.

**How we know.** This is the author's own daily experience, stated in their own words when the project was set up. No other users have been interviewed. The claims that notification overload is general and that it has become worse with AI-driven tools are the author's view and are not measured here.
